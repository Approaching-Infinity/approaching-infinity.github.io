In this episode of Approaching Infinity, host Justin Roopnarine sits down with Rod Loges, Founder and CEO of One Degree Financial, to discuss capital strategy and financial readiness. According to Loges, businesses rarely struggle due to an absolute shortage of capital in the market, but rather because founders fail to build operational readiness before seeking capital or facing an unexpected crisis. He emphasizes that true readiness requires establishing clean financial foundations and developing organizational maturity well in advance.

Loges points out that navigating financing options requires aligning capital choices with long-term enterprise timelines. In his view, commercial bankers have largely shifted from consultative solution providers into product salespeople, leaving unguided founders vulnerable to predatory financing options such as merchant cash advances. Furthermore, Loges cautions that borrowers often overlook the serious legal and personal liabilities associated with government-backed debt, noting that the IRS acts as the ultimate collector when a Small Business Administration loan defaults.

Addressing operational habits, Loges observes that founders frequently get trapped focusing exclusively down and back on daily tasks, much like a Civil War general who declined a machine gun because he was too busy fighting. He explains that getting stuck in the weeds prevents leaders from recognizing high-leverage strategic opportunities above and around them. Loges urges entrepreneurs to intentionally look up and out, maintaining the coachability and perspective required to steer their businesses through unforgiving market conditions.

For early-stage entrepreneurs, Loges advises approaching a new venture as an objective problem-solving project rather than immediately attaching one's personal identity to the business. He stresses that founders should focus first on proving customer demand and validating that their solution addresses a genuine problem people are willing to pay to solve. By maintaining this discipline and aligning financing with a clear end-game strategy, leaders can build durable organizations capable of navigating rapid change.
