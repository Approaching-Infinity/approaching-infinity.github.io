Season 1, Episode 33

In this episode of Approaching Infinity, host Justin Roopnarine speaks with Dr. Arun Seraphin, Executive Director at the Emerging Technologies Institute. While the defense sector has opened more entry points for startups and private capital through innovation hubs like DIU and AFWERX, Dr. Seraphin explains that most commercial innovators still get stuck in the lobby after an initial pilot because they struggle to unlock funded programs of record.

According to Dr. Seraphin, founders frequently mistake enthusiastic feedback from service members in the field for a completed sale, overlooking that the actual acquisition buyers sit in program offices back in Washington. He also emphasizes that commercial startups often focus too narrowly on novel technology while ignoring critical military requirements around reliability, trainability, manufacturability, and repairability in demanding operational environments.

Dr. Seraphin also examines the tension between venture capital exit timelines and long-horizon defense acquisition cycles. To bridge that gap, Dr. Seraphin notes that startups can either focus on mature, rapidly procurable systems or build toward an exit by integrating with and selling to established prime contractors. He adds that defense budgeting often slows down innovation because agencies fund early research and development prototypes without setting aside transition resources for the programs that actually work.

Tune in to hear Dr. Seraphin break down how acquisition policy, public budgeting, and private capital intersect across the modern defense industrial base. This conversation offers practical insights for founders, technologists, and anyone interested in how emerging technologies scale in national security.
