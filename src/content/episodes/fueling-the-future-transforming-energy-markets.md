In this episode, we sit down with John Mossop, Vice President of Technology and Energy Transition at Dril-Quip and CEO of MomentumX. Mossop details his extensive engineering career, starting in the UK before transitioning through General Electric across global industries, including automotive sensing, healthcare, and power. He shares his recent shift from technical engineering roles into commercial product management in the United States, ultimately leading to his current role spearheading energy transition strategies.

Mossop discusses the strategic framework for pivoting traditional oil and gas service companies toward sustainability. He emphasizes the importance of dissecting core organizational capabilities, noting that Drillquip leveraged its fundamental expertise in mechanical engineering, pressure containment, and flow control to identify opportunities in emerging markets. This approach led the company to focus on specific parallels in carbon capture storage, geothermal energy, and hydrogen storage, utilizing a lean, agile team to gather fast feedback from end customers.

The conversation dives into the technical nuances and engineering challenges within these next-generation energy sectors. According to Mossop, the ultimate requirement across CCUS, geothermal, and hydrogen is finding ways to make these technologies economic in comparison to fossil fuels. He explains the distinct difficulties of each, from managing low temperatures and corrosion in carbon storage to handling high pressures and small molecule containment for hydrogen, drawing analogies to how solar and wind industries successfully scaled to reduce costs.

Finally, Mossop reflects on key leadership lessons and the cultural dynamics necessary to facilitate the global energy transition. He highlights a generational shift toward environmental stewardship and the resulting need for organizations to foster a mix of sustainable, affordable energy sources. Mossop advocates for a lean startup culture within established firms, encouraging direct market engagement and constructive dissent to drive innovation. He concludes with his core professional motto for growth: get comfortable with being uncomfortable. 🚀
